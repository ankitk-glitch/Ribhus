The Challenge: Non-Uniform Energy Loads

The building spans 11,073 m² across 12 levels, each with distinct thermal zones and glazing exposure — from a 253 m² basement to a 2,400 m² sixth floor. Energy loads are never uniform across a structure of this scale: a subterranean basement zone exhibits fundamentally different thermodynamic behavior than an extensively glazed upper floor.

Averaging that variation away produces generalized results that cannot be defended under formal audit scrutiny — whether under EPBD, EN ISO 13790, UK Part L, or German GEG standards.

Detailed Fenestration & Solar Exposure

Fenestration is where this building becomes mathematically intricate:

  • 538 Windows & 171 Doors: Ranging from 0.90 × 0.80 m service openings to 3.54 × 2.10 m full-height glazed panels, modeled in their precise positions and dimensions per thermal zone.
  • Glazing Ratios from 7% to 80%: The glazing ratio varies from 7% on lower service levels to 80% on the exposed floor levels.
  • Zone-Level Analysis: Solar gain, overheating risk, and daylight availability were calculated at the specific zone level rather than as a building-wide average — critical for EPBD certification, DGNB assessment, or UK EPC ratings.
