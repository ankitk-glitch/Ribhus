Floor-by-Floor Wall Count & Volume Breakdown

That taper from 351 walls on the 1st floor down to just 38 on the 5th isn't noise — it's the building's real architectural setback, extracted straight from the mesh geometry. For an energy auditor, that means exact envelope surfaces instead of a guess:

  • Basement: 92 walls | 509.70 m³
  • Ground Floor: 292 walls | 2,193.28 m³
  • 1st Floor: 351 walls | 1,572.56 m³
  • 2nd Floor: 324 walls | 1,433.80 m³
  • 3rd Floor: 187 walls | 675.18 m³
  • 4th Floor: 80 walls | 129.90 m³
  • 5th Floor: 38 walls | 81.41 m³
  • Total Envelope Volume: 1,364 walls | 6,595.83 m³

Windows, Doors & Thermal Fenestration

Fenestration is critical for solar gains and daylight calculation:

  • Windows: 602 individual window assemblies totaling 1,114.73 m² of glazing area — ready instantly for daylighting and thermal envelope calculations.
  • Doors: 412 doors, with heights clustered around 2.135 m and widths ranging from 0.75 m up to 1.80 m double entrance assemblies.
  • Zone-by-Zone Attribution: Every single opening is dimensioned and assigned to its correct floor and thermal zone — no averaging, no assumptions.

Why Consultancies Partner with Ribhus

Most BIM outsourcing gets you geometry — walls and rooms that look right on screen but still need re-measuring before an audit can use them. What we deliver is fundamentally different: models built specifically to feed simulation and compliance tools directly, with every wall, opening, and room already zoned, dimensioned, and volume-calculated.

For teams running SAP, PHPP, dynamic thermal simulation, or in-house compliance pipelines, we hand over the underlying IFC, XML, or PLN file itself — so your engineers plug it straight into your existing workflow instead of rebuilding from a flat drawing set. That means less time on data reconstruction and more time on the actual analysis your clients are paying for.
